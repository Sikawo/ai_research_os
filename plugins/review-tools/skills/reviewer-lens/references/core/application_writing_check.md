# Application Writing / English Review

The Application Writing / English Review component is enabled by default in reviewer mode.

This component evaluates whether the draft reads like a strong application. It should not merely proofread. It should identify where language, organization, or tone weakens persuasiveness for a reviewer.

This component inherits Reviewer Lens Locked Mode. Do not praise revised wording
unless it reduces reviewer risk. If the user's intended meaning is good but the
text does not communicate it, say so directly.

## Evaluate

- Grammar
- Syntax
- Clarity
- Paragraph flow
- Logical transitions
- Application-appropriate tone
- Reviewer readability
- Concise but specific wording
- Overclaiming
- Vague claims
- Inflated language
- Whether the text sounds like a fundable research plan rather than a broad essay, review article, or manuscript introduction

## Natural English / Application Prose Pass

Evaluate whether the prose sounds natural to a native English-speaking reviewer while remaining appropriate for a competitive scientific application. The goal is not casual or decorative writing. The goal is prose that sounds like a careful, confident, senior scientist whose first language is English.

Preferred style:

- Natural but not casual
- Formal but not stiff
- Confident but not arrogant
- Clear but not simplistic
- Specific and evidence-based, not generic or promotional
- Concise but not under-explained
- Reviewer-facing rather than self-advertising

Flag AI-like, inflated, or generic language. Risky phrases include:

- groundbreaking
- cutting-edge
- transformative
- revolutionary
- I am deeply passionate about
- I am uniquely positioned
- This project will have broad impact
- This work will fundamentally change

These phrases are not banned, but they require strong evidence. Prefer concrete claims, mechanisms, preliminary evidence, specific achievements, and reviewer-relevant significance.

For each paragraph, check:

- Whether it has a clear paragraph point
- Whether it follows a claim -> evidence -> significance structure
- Whether transitions help non-specialist reviewers follow the argument
- Whether technical terms are introduced before heavy use
- Whether the applicant's contribution is clear without sounding boastful
- Whether caveats are preserved
- Whether the style fits an application rather than a review article, manuscript introduction, or personal essay

Revision behavior:

- Preserve all facts.
- Do not add achievements, preliminary data, claims, or promises.
- Do not exaggerate.
- Preserve uncertainty, feasibility limits, and institutional fit.
- Improve rhythm, word choice, transitions, and reviewer readability.
- Prefer precise verbs over vague intensifiers.
- Replace broad self-praise with concrete evidence.

When reporting an English or prose issue, include:

1. Original phrase or sentence
2. Why it may sound unnatural, inflated, vague, or strategically weak
3. More natural application-style alternative
4. Whether the issue is only English/style or also a reviewer-risk issue

## Reviewer scorecard: natural voice and tone

For every Sprint and GatedSprint review of prose, add two distinct reviewer-facing rows to the score table for each materially different document type:

1. **Natural prose and structure / 文章と構成の自然さ**: assess generic, formulaic or overly smooth claims; interchangeable significance sentences; repetitive transitions, rhythm and sentence patterns; symmetrical Aim or paragraph architecture; vague praise or inflated adjectives that displace the applicant's concrete scientific point.
2. **Confident, proportionate tone / 自信と慎重さのバランス**: assess pre-emptive rebuttals, repeated caveats, apologies, excuses, explanations of why work was not done, or repeated feasibility reassurance that makes a supported claim sound weaker. A necessary limitation stated once, with its consequence or test, is a strength rather than a defect.

Score each row from 5 (strongest) to 1 (weakest) as a **reader impression of the submitted prose**, never an AI-detection probability or claim about authorship:

| Score | Anchor for each row |
| --- | --- |
| 5/5 | Natural and specific throughout; the applicant's scientific reasoning comes through directly, with proportionate caution. No material pattern weakens the reading. |
| 4/5 | Mostly natural and confident; one or two local instances can be tightened without changing the argument. |
| 3/5 | Credible content, but recurring generic phrasing, repeated structure, or defensive framing noticeably weakens the applicant's voice. |
| 2/5 | Frequent patterns obscure the specific contribution or make the case sound apologetic; substantial targeted revision is needed. |
| 1/5 | Pervasive template-like or defensive prose prevents the reviewer from identifying the applicant's specific argument or confident ownership. |
| — | Not assessable: no authorized prose or too little text to judge; outside the ordered scale. |

For each scored row, report score and short label, the document/section, one or two exact short examples (or positive evidence at 5), the concrete reviewer effect, and the smallest safe revision or `keep`. Avoid an unsupported whole-document verdict based on one phrase. If multiple documents differ materially, score them separately; do not average scores into a purported probability of selection. During Sprint, record before and after scores after checking the actual revised prose. During GatedSprint Phase 1, score only the submitted prose and treat alternatives as proposals; re-score the final document in Phase 4 after the approved edits. Ordinary academic wording and needed qualifications are not defects on their own.

## Boundaries

Keep writing issues separate from scientific issues. A sentence may be grammatically correct but strategically weak; a scientific concern may remain even after the prose is polished.

Do not rewrite the entire document unless the user explicitly asks. Provide targeted revision guidance and short examples where helpful.

Writing recommendations should preserve reviewer meaning. Do not smooth away necessary caveats, specific mechanisms, institutional fit, or feasibility constraints just to make the prose more fluent.
