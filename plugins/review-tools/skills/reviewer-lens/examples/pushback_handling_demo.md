# Pushback Handling Demo

This example shows how Reviewer Lens Locked Mode handles user disagreement
without automatically praising the user's argument.

## Initial Reviewer Critique

**A. Locked reviewer verdict**

- Verdict: REVISE
- Confidence: high

**B. Main reviewer risk**

A critical reviewer may worry that the paragraph claims broad disease impact
before showing the mechanism, preliminary evidence, or feasibility path. The idea
may be scientifically plausible, but the current framing asks the reviewer to
accept too much too early.

**D. Reviewer / Editor / Strategist separation**

- Reviewer: the claim currently reads overextended.
- Editor: move the strongest concrete evidence before the broad impact sentence.
- Strategist: keep the ambition, but make the first claim reviewable.

**E. Suggested revision**

"We will test whether [specific mechanism] links [specific biology] to [specific
outcome], establishing a feasible path to evaluate broader disease relevance."

**F. What would change my mind**

Early placement of preliminary evidence, a narrower disease claim, or a direct
mechanistic bridge would lower this risk.

## User Pushback

"But the broader disease implication is the whole point. If I narrow it, the
proposal sounds less ambitious."

## Locked-Mode Response

**C. User pushback / new information assessment**

- Verdict partially updated

Your intent is valid: the proposal should not become timid, and the disease
implication may be important. That updates the strategy, but not the underlying
reviewer concern. The current text still asks the reviewer to accept the broad
claim before seeing the mechanism or evidence.

I would not say the broader framing is better unless it appears after the
reviewer has enough support to see it as earned. The safer strategy is to keep
the ambition but change the order: mechanism first, disease implication second.

**D. Reviewer / Editor / Strategist separation**

- Reviewer: broad impact still reads risky if it appears before support.
- Editor: keep the disease implication, but move it after the concrete mechanism.
- Strategist: ambition is worth preserving; unsupported early overclaiming is
  not.

**F. What would change my mind**

The verdict would become `KEEP` if the paragraph first states the causal
mechanism, names the decision-critical evidence, and then frames the broader
disease relevance as a testable implication rather than a conclusion.
