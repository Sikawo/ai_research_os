# Recommendation Letter Profile

## Purpose

Use this profile when Career Documents Sprint is applied to recommendation
letters, reference letters, letters of support, referee statements, LoR drafts,
recommendation drafts, or similar documents.

Recommendation letters need separate handling from resumes, CVs, and cover
letters because the main risks are recommender voice, relationship credibility,
factual boundaries, applicant-authored tone, unsupported praise, overclaiming,
and bias-coded or vague language.

## Core Objective

Produce a credible, target-aware recommendation letter that sounds like it was
written by the recommender, preserves factual accuracy, explains the
recommender-applicant relationship, and supports praise with evidence the
recommender could plausibly know.

The profile must not turn a letter into a CV summary or an applicant-authored
self-promotion document.

## Source-Based Letter-Writing Principles

When official or high-authority guidance is available, Recommendation Letter
Sprint should incorporate the principles without pasting long excerpts.

- Program-specific instructions override generic recommendation-letter advice.
- A recommendation letter should not simply summarize the applicant's CV.
- The recommender-applicant relationship should be explicit.
- Specific observations and evidence are stronger than generic praise.
- The letter should explain why the applicant fits the target opportunity.
- The letter should remain concise and focused unless the target program clearly
  warrants a longer letter.
- The review should audit for vague praise, unsupported superlatives, bias-coded
  language, and applicant-authored or AI-like phrasing.

## Required Inputs

Use only user-authorized source material. Helpful inputs include:

- target opportunity, program, role, or fellowship;
- official recommendation-letter instructions, if available;
- draft recommendation letter, if available;
- recommender identity and role;
- recommender-applicant relationship, including duration and context;
- evidence directly observed by the recommender;
- CV-supported facts the recommender is allowed to reference;
- user-provided facts that need confirmation before strong wording;
- target length, format, or submission constraints.

If key relationship or evidence details are missing, mark them as `missing`
instead of inventing them.

## Recommendation Letter Review Axes

Review recommendation letters across these axes:

1. target fit
2. recommender credibility
3. relationship clarity
4. evidence strength
5. claim boundary / overclaim risk
6. recommender voice
7. applicant-authored or AI-like phrasing
8. bias / vague praise / unsupported superlatives
9. length and structure
10. missing information

## Voice Rules

The letter should sound like a credible senior academic or professional
recommender.

Prefer:

- specific but measured praise;
- evidence-based judgment;
- clear relationship statements;
- concrete observations;
- natural professional language;
- restrained enthusiasm where appropriate.

Avoid:

- overly generic openings such as "It is with great enthusiasm..." unless
  natural for the recommender;
- "unique combination of...";
- "passion for science";
- "stands at the forefront";
- "transformative potential" unless strongly justified;
- too many abstract nouns in one sentence;
- repeated "independent", "innovative", "strong", or "excellent" without
  evidence;
- phrasing that sounds like the applicant is self-promoting;
- language that overstates the recommender's direct knowledge.

## Drafting Logic

A strong recommendation letter should usually follow this structure:

1. Opening recommendation and target
2. Recommender identity and relationship to applicant
3. Personal observation of applicant
4. Scientific, professional, or academic contributions known to the recommender
5. Growth, independence, judgment, or leadership potential
6. Target-specific fit
7. Awards or external recognition, if relevant
8. Closing recommendation

Do not invent anecdotes. Do not invent direct supervision, coauthorship,
collaboration, rankings, or personal knowledge. Do not change publication,
award, employment, degree, visa, or citizenship status.

## Evidence Labels

Use these labels when drafting, reviewing, or warning about claims:

- `official requirement`: from the target program, application portal, or other
  official instruction.
- `public background`: public information about the target opportunity,
  program, institution, or field context.
- `user-provided`: facts supplied by the user but not independently verified in
  the current review.
- `CV-supported`: facts supported by an authorized CV or resume.
- `recommender-observed`: facts or judgments the recommender directly observed
  or could plausibly know from the stated relationship.
- `inference`: a reasoned conclusion from available evidence.
- `uncertain`: plausible but not confirmed.
- `missing`: needed evidence is not available in the current context.

Distinguish direct observation from CV-supported facts, user-provided facts,
inference, uncertainty, and missing evidence.

## Editing Policy

Low-risk edits may:

- improve concision;
- remove AI-like language;
- improve flow;
- align tone with the recommender;
- clarify the relationship;
- soften overclaims;
- replace generic praise with evidence-based praise;
- reduce CV-summary passages when they do not add recommender-specific
  judgment.

High-tradeoff edits must be listed as human-decision proposals. These include:

- adding anecdotes;
- adding direct ranking;
- changing the strength of endorsement;
- adding claims of supervision, collaboration, or personal knowledge;
- mentioning sensitive personal information;
- changing publication, award, employment, degree, visa, or citizenship status;
- making a letter longer than the target length;
- adding claims that depend on private or uncertain facts.

## Output Contract

Recommendation Letter Sprint should produce:

1. revised letter
2. short change report
3. overclaim / credibility warnings
4. bias / vague praise / unsupported superlative warnings
5. missing-information list
6. optional shorter version if the draft exceeds the target length

The output must preserve factual boundaries, label weak or missing evidence, and
avoid inflated, generic, or AI-like praise.
